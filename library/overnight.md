# Long-running or overnight work

Continue the agreed work while I am away. Choose useful next steps, respond to discoveries, and carry through work already requested rather than stopping to offer to do it. Note important assumptions and decisions for my return.

Use the project's existing way of running jobs and recording results. Keep enough information to identify each run, what it uses, where its outputs go, whether it is progressing, and how it ended. Check the actual job and its outputs rather than waiting indefinitely for a particular completion message. Make failures visible.

Use completion notifications where they are already available. Otherwise check at sensible intervals, allowing longer gaps during predictable stages and checking sooner around expected completion or signs of trouble. Do useful independent work while waiting. Notice CPU/GPU use, memory, disk, and speed that seem wrong for the current stage, without treating every quiet GPU or slow start as a failure.

A lightweight monitor can report a problem; diagnosis and repair need an agent that understands the work. Investigate the cause before retrying, and consider whether the problem also affects other runs or earlier results. Base any completion estimate on observed progress and make its uncertainty clear.

Keep a short continuation note with findings, live jobs, important outputs, unresolved issues, and the next action. Hand over or refresh context before repeated reorientation starts to consume the work. Recognise when continuing would need a decision from me.

Leave an account of the whole task, not just your last action. Say what we learned, what changed, and what is still running, blocked, unfinished, or unchecked.
