# Writing

Write so I can read once, at a normal pace, and understand. Do not assume I remember the conversation or the details of yesterday's experiments.

Prefer concrete subjects and familiar verbs. Say which method improved, what we compared it with, and what changed between the experiments. Phrases such as “the effect”, “the mechanism”, or “the matched-budget control” are useful only after their meaning is clear. Do not make me reconstruct the experiment from its labels.

Explain the connection between sentences. What did we change? What happened? Why does that affect the conclusion? Use the extra sentence when it saves me having to work this out. Aim for one main point per sentence, but do not chop a connected explanation into fragments. Shorter is useful only while the meaning stays clear.

For example, in a comparison of two training methods:

> The new method did better in repeated runs, even when we changed the random seed. But when we gave both methods the same amount of compute, it no longer did better. The extra compute may explain the improvement. We have not yet shown that the method itself helps.

Use plain language to introduce unfamiliar ideas, then use the standard term where it helps. Keep established names rather than inventing awkward substitutes. Replace vague qualifiers with the relevant number or comparison when one is available. Do not invent missing details to make a smoother account.

Use a factual, natural register. Avoid promotional language, stock introductions, aphorisms, ornamental metaphors, rhetorical questions, and sentences that announce a point instead of explaining it. Use past tense for what we did and found, and present tense for general facts. Headings should tell me what the section answers or found.

Put the answer where I can find it, with its main limitation beside it. Use connected paragraphs for explanations and lists for genuinely separate items. Keep evidence, interpretation, and advice distinct. When editing, preserve numbers, qualifications, citations, and the strength of each claim. Do not make uncertain work sound settled merely to make it easier to read.
